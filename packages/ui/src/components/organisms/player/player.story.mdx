The whole chrome, assembled. `Player.Root` owns no playback of its own: it is handed a **`PlayerController`** and a `<Player.Media>`, and everything it draws (the top bar, the chapter bar, the transport, the panels, the up-next sheet) reads from that one object. That is what lets the same component draw over four different decoders (an in-page `<video>` on the web; AVPlay, mpv or ExoPlayer rendering to a native plane *behind* a transparent page on the televisions). `flags` is the other half: a TV has no volume slider, no PiP and no fullscreen, so those controls are absent rather than disabled.

The chrome is composed, not configured. A part is a direct child, and there are two kinds. A **face** draws its children where the Root puts them: `Media`, `Title`, `Subtitle`, `Warning`, `Actions`, `Panel`. A **setting** draws nothing and carries what a region of the Root's own furniture needs: `Transport`, `UpNext`, `Credits`, `PostPlay`, `SkipIntro`, `Subtitles`, `Report`. Write a setting and that region appears; leave it out and it does not. The transport row is the Root's rather than a slot a caller fills, because a row anyone could rearrange would break D-pad traversal.

`title` stays on the Root and draws nothing: it names what is playing for assistive tech and for the end card, and `<Player.Title>` is what puts it on screen.

```tsx
<Player.Root
  controller={controller}
  flags={WEB_FLAGS}
  title={item.title}
  onClose={({ reason }) => leave(reason)}
>
  <Player.Media>
    <video ref={videoRef} />
  </Player.Media>
  <Player.Title>{item.title}</Player.Title>
  <Player.Transport chapters={chapters} tileAt={storyboard.tileAt} />
  <Player.Subtitles appearance={appearance} onAppearanceChange={setAppearance} gen={subtitleGen} />
  <Player.UpNext data={upNext} onPlay={play} />
</Player.Root>
```

<Do>
- Pass the platform `flags` (`WEB_FLAGS` / `TV_FLAGS`) rather than branching inside a screen.
- Hand it a controller: the chrome must never reach for the video element itself.
- Give it the whole stage: it measures itself and sizes its chrome to what it gets.
- Read `reason` on `onClose`: `back` is the remote, `close` the chrome's own control.
</Do>

<Dont>
- Render two. It owns the remote, the key handling and the fullscreen element.
- Wrap it in a fixed-width box: the chrome would size itself for a stage it isn't on.
- Wrap `<Player.Media>` in a box of your own: the surface must stay a direct child of the stage.
- Reach for a prop where a part exists: the top bar's words are `<Player.Title>`, not `title`.
</Dont>
