The edge-anchored slide-in sheet: a detail inspector, an edit form, the phone
nav. The same overlay contract as **Dialog** - portalled, scroll-locked, focus
locked behind it, Esc/Back and outside-press dismiss, `role="dialog"` with
`title` as its accessible name - but anchored to a side with its own enter/exit
slide. It stays mounted through the exit, so callers just flip `open`.

## Three bands, one scroller

The sheet is made of three bands, and only the middle one scrolls:

| Band | Scrolls | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Drawer.Header` | no | the title, and the way out |
| `Drawer.Panel` | yes | the body, however long it gets |
| `Drawer.Footer` | no | a shelf for the sheet's actions |

`Drawer.Close` is the way out, and it reads the Root's `onClose` rather than
taking one: a header of your own keeps the button the default one draws, and a
sheet that cannot be dismissed draws nothing without you writing the condition.
It takes the rest of an icon button's props, so a sheet whose header already
carries controls can match their size.

`Panel` scrolls through a `ScrollView`, so a sheet written once works on a
television and a phone as well as in a browser. A pane of the caller's own -
a `<div>` with `overflow-y`, say - is the one thing that will not.

The pinned bands draw their own hairline against the panel, so a sheet is
three parts rather than five.

## Naming it, then arranging it

`title` names the sheet to assistive tech, and draws the ordinary header, close
button included:

```tsx
<Drawer.Root open={open} onClose={close} title="Edit registry">
  <Field.Root label="Name" />
</Drawer.Root>
```

Reach for the parts when the sheet needs a header of its own:

```tsx
<Drawer.Root open={open} onClose={close} title={report.subjectTitle}>
  <Drawer.Header>
    <Row between>
      <Text variant="overline">Report</Text>
      <Drawer.Close />
    </Row>
    <Text variant="h2">{report.subjectTitle}</Text>
  </Drawer.Header>
  <Drawer.Panel>…</Drawer.Panel>
  <Drawer.Footer>…</Drawer.Footer>
</Drawer.Root>
```

A part counts only as a **direct child** of the Root. A header assembled by a
component of your own is wrapped at the call site
(`<Drawer.Header><MyHeader /></Drawer.Header>`) rather than returning
`<Drawer.Header>` from inside.
