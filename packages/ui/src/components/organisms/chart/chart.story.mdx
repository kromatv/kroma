A plot of one or more series over one shared scale. The Root owns the box, the scale and the paint, and it reads what to draw off its own direct children: a mark goes into the drawing, a scale reserves room beside it, the key sits underneath. Only the SAMPLES are a `data` prop, which is DESIGN.md's T3 (provenance): the points arrive off the wire as a metrics snapshot or a play-history bucket, so writing them as children would mean writing a JSX interpreter over a parsed array. T5 seconds it, because a caller could arrange the samples out of order and a scale drawn against a shuffled series lies rather than fails. The SHAPE stays composed, which is why every mark, scale, key and readout is a part. A plot is a figure and not a control: it takes no D-pad stop, and everything the readout says is also in the Root's `label`. A trace is smoothed by default with a monotone cubic, because everything this product plots is a continuous quantity sampled at an interval rather than a set of discrete counts, and a monotone cubic is the one smoothing that cannot leave the range of the two samples it joins: a metric resting at 0.0 never dips below it. Take `linear` where the samples are discrete counts, and `step` where a value is held until the next sample replaces it.

```tsx
<Chart.Root data={samples} x="at" height={208} format={mbps} label="Throughput, 8 Mb/s now">
  <Chart.Grid />
  <Chart.Area series="local" label="Local" />
  <Chart.Line series="remote" label="Remote" />
  <Chart.Axis edge="left" />
  <Chart.Axis edge="bottom" />
  <Chart.Tooltip />
  <Chart.Legend />
  <Chart.Footer>Average 5.2 remote, 5.1 local</Chart.Footer>
</Chart.Root>
```

<Do>
- Give a plot that carries information a `label` saying what it shows and how it is going: assistive tech cannot read a drawing, and a plot without one is treated as decoration and hidden.
- Let a series take the palette slot its position earns. Name a colour only where the role is already learned, the way amber is local traffic on the dashboard.
- Pin `min` and `max` where the scale is a percentage, so a quiet hour does not draw like a busy one.
- Write a `<Chart.Tooltip>` to make the plot follow a pointer at all: without one it draws and does nothing else.
- Reach for `curve="step"` where a sample is a level that HELD until the next one, and for `linear` where a value between two samples would be an invention.
</Do>

<Dont>
- Run past three series in one plot. The data palette has three slots and no fourth hue separates under CVD, so facet into a second chart instead.
- Put an alpha on a series' paint. A wash is drawn at an opacity under an opaque trace, which composites once rather than per crossing.
- Wrap a part: only a DIRECT child of the Root takes part, because the Root sorts its children to know what belongs where.
- Stack two scales on one edge. Two units in one plot is two plots.
- Reach for a smoother curve than `monotone`. Anything that overshoots draws a value no sample reached, which on a metric that cannot go negative is a lie the reader has no way to see.
</Dont>
