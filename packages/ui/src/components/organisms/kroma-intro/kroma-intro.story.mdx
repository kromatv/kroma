The brand film the apps open with (every TV, the desktop and the phone, but not the browser client): a chromatic light tunnel landing on the KR-wheel-MA lockup, full-screen with sound. It ships twice: the 4K60 HEVC master, and a 720p60 H.264 encode for every engine without an HEVC decoder (Firefox, Chrome without hardware HEVC, an older TV). The `<video>` element plays the first one it can decode. When it can decode neither, the intro hands off to the app at once.

It is a DOM component, deliberately: a real `<video>`, `document` visibility handling, and inline styles rather than a framework so it renders identically on the SSR shell and on a 2017 TV webview. On native it renders `null`. The Apple TV, Android TV and phone clients play the same film through `expo-video` in their own shell (a phone held upright plays a 1080x1920 cut of it), and fall back to the same H.264 encode when the device reports it cannot decode HEVC. That is why this story is a note rather than a film in the native workbench.
