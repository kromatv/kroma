One selected option among a few, all visible at once. It is the choice a dropdown would hide. A **radiogroup** to assistive tech: each segment is a radio carrying its checked state, and on a physical keyboard the arrow keys move the selection the way a native radio group does. Composed, in Radix's shape: **Root** owns the value and the group semantics, **Item** is one segment, **Label** is its drawn title and the segment's accessible name, and **Hint** is the quieter word beside it for the fact that settles the choice.

```tsx
<SegmentGroup.Root label="Playback mode" value={mode} onValueChange={setMode}>
  <SegmentGroup.Item value="auto">
    <SegmentGroup.Label>Auto</SegmentGroup.Label>
    <SegmentGroup.Hint>Recommended</SegmentGroup.Hint>
  </SegmentGroup.Item>
  <SegmentGroup.Item value="direct">
    <SegmentGroup.Label>Direct play</SegmentGroup.Label>
  </SegmentGroup.Item>
</SegmentGroup.Root>

// A glyph-only segment has no Label to read, so `label` carries its name:
<SegmentGroup.Item value="grid" icon="layout-grid" label="Grid" />
```

<Do>
- Use it for two to four options that fit on one line.
- Name the group with `label`. It is what a screen reader announces first.
- Give a glyph-only Item a `label`: the glyph carries no words to read.
</Do>

<Dont>
- Reach for it past four options. That is a `<Select>`.
- Use it for an on/off pair. That is a `<Switch>`.
</Dont>
